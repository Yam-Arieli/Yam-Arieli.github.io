Across all three

Wearing all three hats

Some of the work was assigned; a lot of it wasn't. Taking what a manager hands you is half the role, the other half is noticing the question people keep working around, the need nobody has filed, and making the case for it clearly enough that someone acts on it. Most of the projects I care about started there.

Once one started, it rarely stayed inside one of the three roles above. In the same week I might sit with users to pin down what they needed, train and validate a model, and fix an ETL that broke overnight. Moving between them is most of the job, and it is what keeps a problem from falling into the gap between one role and the next.

  1. 01Notice the gap. No one assigns these; they're found by paying attention to what keeps going unanswered.
  2. 02Propose the question. Turn a vague frustration into a claim that could be shown false.
  3. 03Make the data exist. Source it, collect it, resolve it, validate it, usually from nothing.
  4. 04Establish it's real. Null models before methods; kill the idea early if it doesn't survive.
  5. 05Model it. The smallest model that is both accurate and explainable to a non-specialist.
  6. 06Ship & monitor. Into the operational environment, with drift checks and a fallback.
  7. 07Brief the decision. Directly to the people acting on it, with the reasoning attached.
  8. 08Sit with the users. Watch it in use; the next gap is almost always visible from there.
